Purdue ECE 20007 · Power Electronics Design Projects

A boost converter and an equalizer, measured against their simulations

Jan 2026 to May 2026 · West Lafayette, Indiana

Two breadboard circuits, each designed on paper, simulated in LTspice, built, and measured. The interesting part is not that they worked; it is where the bench disagreed with the simulation and why.

The interactive version of this page is being built. What is here now is the summary and the schematics.

What I did

  • Designed, built and measured an adjustable DC-DC boost converter stepping a 5V input to a 5V to 30V range, sizing the switching duty cycle from the boost transfer function rather than tuning it empirically. A PMOS output ceiling limiter holds the rail under no load or a shorted load, a failure mode that otherwise destroys the output capacitors.
  • Reconciled measured output against LTspice by accounting for wire and breadboard resistance, component tolerance, and device on-resistance.
  • Designed a three-band analog audio equalizer with active RC filters, LM324 op-amp buffers placed to remove loading between the filter and volume stages, per-band inverting amplifiers for gain, and a power amplifier stage driving over 400 mW into 8 ohms.
