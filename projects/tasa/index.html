Taiwan Space Agency · Satellite Electronics Intern

Pointing a satellite with spinning wheels

Jul 2026 to Aug 2026 · Hsinchu, Taiwan

A satellite has nothing to push against. To turn it, you spin a wheel inside it one way and the body turns the other. I built the bench electronics and the flight firmware that command four such wheels, and the simulator that predicted what they would do, then put the two together on an air bearing stage.

The interactive version of this page is being built. What is here now is the summary and the photographs.

What I did

  • Designed the bench test board for reaction wheel validation in KiCad and built it, integrating an ESP32-S3 microcontroller, a 9-axis IMU over I2C, an RS422 transceiver, and a DC-DC buck converter with hardware and software safety interlocks.
  • Wrote 10,000 lines of embedded C and C++ attitude control firmware across 30 modules for an ESP32-S3, implementing the RS-422 actuator protocol, a BNO055 IMU driver, dual-transport operator telemetry, and autonomous fault detection, isolation and recovery. Cut combined communication and motor latency from 727 ms to 263 ms.
  • Verified the RS422 serial protocol byte by byte against oscilloscope-captured traffic, diagnosing four firmware defects including memory corruption behind a reboot loop.
  • Built a 20,000-line, 157-module MATLAB simulation with 100 testbenches covering rigid-body dynamics, quaternion control, pyramid torque allocation and orbital disturbances, then validated it against an air-bearing physical twin across 65 hardware-in-the-loop runs.
  • Delivered an actuator sizing and trade study across 8 parameters, 12 metrics and 6 slewing maneuvers, documented in a 768-paragraph technical report with 21 tables and 40 figures, plus an A1 conference poster.
