Decoding bits that moved

May 2026 to Jul 2026 · Hsinchu, Taiwan

Classical error correction assumes every bit stays in its slot. Delete one bit and every bit after it shifts, so a decoder that compares position by position sees dozens of errors where there was one. I benchmarked machine learning decoders against that channel.

The interactive version of this page is being built. What is here now is the summary.

What I did

  • Applied Transformer architectures to error-correction coding over synchronization-error channels, where an insertion or deletion shifts every following bit and classical decoders lose the position assumption they rest on.
  • Built three designs in PyTorch on CUDA: an autoregressive encoder-decoder, a Siamese encoder learning a 256-dimensional space where Euclidean distance tracks edit distance, and a CTC head decoding bits directly.
  • Cut edit-distance estimation error to 2.09 RMSE against 2.62 and 2.91 for the CGK and CNN-ED baselines, with BCJR as the classical reference, across 1M binary codewords.
